## What is a Wilson CI?

A **Wilson 95% confidence interval** is a range we can claim — with 95% confidence — contains the variant's *true* win rate, given the wins and losses we've seen so far.

Why not just use `wins / (wins+loss)`?

That raw hit rate is the **point estimate**. With small samples it's wildly noisy:
- 1 win / 0 losses → 100% hit rate (but it's one trade)
- 16 wins / 1 loss → 94% hit rate (much more trustworthy)
- 3 wins / 3 losses → 50% hit rate (but could plausibly be a 20% variant having a lucky streak)

Wilson CIs collapse that into a range. A variant with a Wilson CI of **[73%, 99%]** is statistically very different from one with **[19%, 81%]** — even if both have the same point estimate.

The two numbers that matter:
- **Lower bound > 50%** → variant is statistically beating a coin flip (real edge)
- **Lower bound > random benchmark** → wide enough sample that the edge isn't sample noise

Wilson is preferred over the naive normal-approximation interval because it stays inside [0,1] and behaves well even when `p` is near 0 or 1 or `n` is small — exactly our regime.

Formula: `center = (p + z²/2n) / (1 + z²/n)`, half-width = `z·√(p(1-p)/n + z²/4n²) / (1 + z²/n)` with z=1.96 for 95%.
